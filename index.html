    How to Use Your Program

    A quick guide so you always know what to do — no need to message between sessions.

    How to build a split (choosing your days)

    A "split" is how you divide the body across your training days. Pick days that together cover the whole body over the week. Here's what each day trains:

    PushChest, shoulders & triceps
    PullBack, biceps & rear delts
    Legs / LowerQuads, hamstrings, glutes & calves
    UpperChest, back, shoulders & arms
    Full BodyA bit of everything in one session
    Back & Biceps · Chest & Shoulders · ArmsSingle "bro-split" focus days

    Most popular splits:

    • Full Body (3 days/week) — best for beginners or a busy schedule; every session hits everything.
    • Upper / Lower (4 days) — alternate an upper-body day and a lower-body day. Great all-rounder.
    • Push / Pull / Legs (3–6 days) — the gym favorite. Run it once through for 3 days, or twice for 6.
    • Bro Split (5 days) — one muscle group per day: chest, back, shoulders, legs, arms.

    What pairs together: Push + Pull + Legs · Upper + Lower · or just repeat Full Body. Don't double up — if you pick Push, you don't also need Chest & Shoulders (Push already covers them). The picker nudges you as you choose. Every program needs at least one lower-body day.

    How to read sets & reps

    "4 × 8–12" means 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps. Pick a weight where the last couple of reps are tough but your form stays clean. Tempo like "3s down" means take 3 seconds to lower the weight. Rest is how long to wait between sets.

    Grp A1 / A2 = a superset — do them back-to-back, then rest. A1 / A2 / A3 = a triset (three moves in a row).

    How to progress week to week

    Each week, aim to beat the last — add a rep or a little weight while keeping your form clean. Your plan's rep targets climb as the weeks go on. When a weight feels easy at the top of the rep range, go up a small amount next time. Run a program for 4–6 weeks, then switch it up.

    What to do if something hurts

    Mild muscle fatigue and a good "burn" are normal. Sharp or joint pain is a stop sign — stop that exercise, swap it (see below), and let me know. Never push through joint pain.

    How to swap an exercise

    If you don't have the equipment, or a movement bothers you, use a swap below. In the app you can also pick any exercise from the dropdown on each row — it only ever shows options that are safe for your gym and conditions.

    How to track your progress

    Use the Trackers tab to log your bodyweight, personal records (PRs), daily habits, and any aches in the mobility/pain log. Tracking is the fastest way to see the work paying off.
